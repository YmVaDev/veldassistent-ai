Goal: Create an illustration that looks like it belongs to a premium European wildlife field guide. The emphasis is on simplicity, scientific accuracy, and consistency, not realism.

Style requirements:
Scientifically accurate anatomy, proportions, posture and natural colours.
Instantly recognizable as the correct species.
Extremely simplified design using only 6–10 large, clean colour shapes.
No outlines.
No gradients.
No shadows.
No textures.
No feather, fur or scale details.
Smooth organic vector shapes with rounded transitions.
Calm, balanced composition.
Modern Scandinavian-style flat illustration.

Biological accuracy:
Depict the requested sex, life stage and seasonal appearance accurately.
If not specified, illustrate the most characteristic form for species identification.
Use breeding plumage or breeding colours where appropriate.
Use the aquatic breeding phase for amphibians whenever applicable.

Species specific behavior:
Depict the species in its most characteristic natural posture or behaviour, while maintaining the same composition and illustration style.
Examples
• Woodpeckers: clinging vertically to a tree trunk.
• Nuthatches: climbing head-first down a tree trunk.
• Kingfisher: perched on a branch above water.
• Swallows: perched on a wire 
• Herons: standing in shallow water.
• Owls: perched upright on a fence post facing the front.
• Ducks: floating on water.
• Grebes: swimming.
• Waders: standing in shallow water.
• Raptors: perched on a fence post.
• Larks and pipits: standing on the ground.
• Tits, finches and sparrows: perched on a simple branch.
Other species groups:
No branch!

Whenever appropriate, depict the species with a simple element that reinforces its natural ecology or identification, provided it does not distract from the subject.

Examples
• Owl with a small mouse.
• Peregrine Falcon with a small passerine.
• Common Kestrel with a vole.
• White-tailed Eagle with a fish.
• Kingfisher with a small fish.
• Grey Heron with a fish.
• Bee-eater with a bee.
• Green Woodpecker on the ground with an ant.
• Great Spotted Woodpecker on a tree trunk.
• Fox carrying a vole.
• Otter with a fish.
The ecological element should remain secondary and highly simplified. It must never compete visually with the main subject. 

Composition grid:
• Use the same composition across the entire collection.
• Square canvas (1:1).
• Subject centered.
• The animal should occupy approximately 75–80% of the canvas.
• Leave generous white space around the subject.
• Place the subject inside a soft light grey circle of identical size in every illustration.
• Keep the eye approximately at the same height within the composition whenever possible.
• Maintain consistent margins on all sides.
• Use the same viewing angle for comparable species.
• Depict each species in the viewing angle that best highlights its defining identification features while maintaining a consistent composition across the collection.
The side profile is always facing left.

Preferred viewpoints
• Songbirds, pigeons, woodpeckers, kingfishers, bee-eaters, rollers: side profile.
• Owls: frontal or slightly three-quarter frontal view.
• Birds of prey: frontal or slightly three-quarter frontal view when perched.
• Waterfowl: side profile.
• Waders: side profile.
• Herons and egrets: side profile.
• Swifts and swallows: side profile or characteristic flight posture.
• Woodpeckers: clinging vertically to a tree trunk.
• Nuthatches: climbing head-first down a tree trunk.
• Creepers: spiralling upwards on a tree trunk.
• Perches, branches and tree trunks should be simple and unobtrusive, serving only to support the subject.
• The overall visual weight should remain consistent throughout the collection.

Collection consistency:
Simplify the species to its essential identifying shapes while preserving scientific recognizability.
Prioritize clean silhouette over detail. Every colour shape should have a clear purpose. If a detail does not improve species recognition, omit it.
When simplifying a species, never simplify by removing its defining characteristics. Instead, merge details into larger, cleaner colour shapes.

Quality requirements:
Avoid common AI illustration artifacts.
Do not add unnecessary details, decorative shapes or stylization.
Every colour block must contribute to species recognition.
Prefer fewer, larger shapes over many small ones.
Keep edges clean and intentional.
The illustration should feel timeless, calm and handcrafted rather than AI-generated.

Reference image:
Use the attached image only as a style reference. Match the illustration style, proportions, colour treatment and level of simplification exactly. Do not copy the species.
